2. Introduction

    Final Year Project (FYP) management is a multi-stage academic process involving
proposal processing, project allocation, collaboration, project completion, evaluation,
and administrative coordination. Prior FYP-management research has shown that these
activities benefit from centralized digital workflows and role-specific collaboration tools
rather than disconnected manual processes [1, 2]. In many university environments,
forms, spreadsheets, emails, messaging applications, and loosely organized digital records
can make proposal tracking, supervisor coordination, evaluation, and reuse of previous
project knowledge difficult.

    FYPHub is proposed as a centralized, role-aware, AI-assisted platform for managing
the complete FYP lifecycle. The platform is designed for five major roles: Student, Super-
visor, FYP Coordinator, Panel Member, and Administrator. It combines deterministic
academic rules with Large Language Models (LLMs), semantic embeddings, Retrieval-
Augmented Generation (RAG), GraphRAG, machine-learning based risk analysis, opti-
mization techniques, and agentic AI orchestration. RAG provides language models with
retrieved external evidence for knowledge-intensive generation, while GraphRAG extends
retrieval with graph-structured relationships for broader and multi-hop reasoning over
document collections [3, 4]. AI is used for assistance, analysis, retrieval, explanation, and
prediction, while final academic authority remains with authorized university personnel.

    The FYP is organized into twelve clearly separated modules. Each module owns a
distinct part of the FYP lifecycle so that proposal management, quality compliance, orig-
inality checking, supervision, scheduling, evaluation, progress monitoring, institutional
knowledge retrieval, and final archiving do not overlap unnecessarily.

3. Project Description

    FYPHub is a responsive web-based platform that digitizes and intelligently connects
the complete Final Year Project process. Students can form groups, find compatible
teammates, create and submit proposal versions, run AI-assisted compliance checks, com-
pare proposals against previous FYPs, and request suitable supervisors. Supervisors can
review proposals using structured comments and revision requests, while the Coordina-
tor manages academic routing and panel readiness. The system also schedules defenses,
supports structured panel evaluation, tracks project progress and supervision evidence,
archives completed FYPs, and makes institutional project knowledge searchable through
RAG and GraphRAG.

    The platform follows a shared-module, role-based-access architecture. A feature is not
duplicated into separate modules merely because different roles can view it. For example,
the proposal compliance engine remains one module, but students, supervisors, coordina-
tors, and panel members access different views according to their permissions. Similarly,
the final archive creates and preserves institutional knowledge, while the knowledge-search
module retrieves and reasons over that archived information.

    The system is expected to provide a single source of truth for proposal versions,
supervisor assignments, review comments, coordinator actions, panel evaluations, project
progress, and final project outcomes. This improves traceability and supports data-driven
academic decision making across the entire FYP lifecycle.

4. Real World Problem

    Universities generate a large amount of valuable FYP knowledge every semester, but
that knowledge is often difficult to search, compare, and reuse. Students may not know
whether an idea has already been implemented, what limitations previous projects had,
which supervisor is most suitable, or which university formatting and proposal require-
ments are still unmet. Group formation can also be difficult when students do not have
suitable teammates, and duplicate membership can occur if group rules are not centrally
enforced.

    FYP administration also involves several academic actors. Students submit propos-
als, supervisors review them, coordinators control academic routing, panels evaluate the
work, and administrators configure academic rules and deadlines. When these activities
are distributed across disconnected tools, important context is lost. Revision comments
can become scattered, supervisor capacity may be unclear, panel scheduling can create
conflicts, progress evidence may remain informal, and completed projects can become
static files rather than reusable institutional knowledge.

    The real-world challenge is therefore broader than simple proposal submission. The
university needs an integrated system that manages the workflow, applies deterministic
academic rules, provides AI-assisted analysis where appropriate, preserves an auditable
history, and converts historical FYP records into an active knowledge base for future
students and faculty.

5. Problem Statement

    Current FYP management practices may suffer from fragmented team formation,
manual proposal submission, inconsistent formatting, weak originality checks, limited
visibility of supervisor expertise and capacity, scattered review communication, man-
ual defense scheduling, disconnected panel feedback, limited progress evidence, and
poor reuse of completed-project knowledge. Existing generic learning-management,
plagiarism-detection, and project-management tools can support individual parts of this
process, but they do not provide a unified FYP-specific platform that connects academic
workflow, proposal intelligence, supervisor allocation, panel evaluation, project progress,
and institutional knowledge.

    The proposed project aims to develop the FYPHub platform, which manages the
complete FYP lifecycle through twelve integrated but non-overlapping modules. The
system will combine role-based workflow control with AI-assisted proposal compliance,
semantic originality analysis, supervisor matching, revision verification, scheduling opti-
mization, progress and risk intelligence, RAG-based institutional knowledge search, and
final project handover support.

    FYPHub introduces several capabilities that are not normally available together in a
single FYP management platform:

    · AI-assisted teammate discovery using compatibility questions, skills, availability,
       work style, and role preferences, with duplicate-group membership prevention.

    · University-template-aware proposal compliance checking for structure, headings,
       margins, spacing, alignment, required sections, and academic consistency.

    · Semantic and functional comparison of new proposals against archived FYPs, in-
       cluding contribution boundaries and extension gaps.

    · Expertise, workload, preference, and capacity-based supervisor recommendation
       and allocation with slot enforcement.

    · Structured supervisor comments, revision requests, two-way discussion, and AI-
       assisted verification of whether requested revisions were resolved.

    · Coordinator-controlled academic routing with delay and workflow-conformance
       monitoring.

    · Conflict-aware defense scheduling and panel assignment based on availability,
       room/time constraints, and academic conflicts.

    · Panel scoring and feedback with AI-generated viva questions, evaluator disagree-
       ment detection, and feedback-resolution tracking.

    · Supervision-meeting evidence, milestone tracking, project-health classification,
       delay-risk analysis, and recovery recommendations.

    · Final FYP archiving with implemented-scope extraction, limitations, future work,
       reusable assets, and intelligent handover packages.

    · RAG/GraphRAG-based institutional FYP search with evidence and multi-hop rea-
       soning over archived projects.

    · Institutional analytics for domain trends, workload, turnaround time, completion
       patterns, and management insights.

8. Application Review

    This section reviews six existing platforms that address selected parts of the FYP
lifecycle. Each application is summarized through two key characteristics and two limita-
tions, followed by a feature-by-application comparison table in the same tabular pattern
used throughout this proposal. The purpose is to show which capabilities already exist
in established systems and which FYP-specific gaps remain.
8.1 Moodle Assignment Activity [5]

    Moodle provides structured assignment submission, group submissions, deadlines,
grading, feedback, and rubric-based assessment within a learning-management environ-
ment.

    · Key characteristic 1: Students can submit files or online text individually or in
       groups, with configurable availability and due dates.

    · Key characteristic 2: Instructors can grade submissions, provide feedback, and
       use rubrics or custom grading scales.

The limitations of Moodle for the target FYP lifecycle are listed below.
    · Limitation 1: It does not provide FYP-specific supervisor expertise/capacity
       matching, allocation optimization, or defense-panel scheduling.

    · Limitation 2: It does not provide institutional FYP semantic originality analysis,
       GraphRAG reasoning, or intelligent handover/continuation support.

8.2 Turnitin Similarity Report [6]

    Turnitin compares submitted text against large source collections and produces a Sim-
ilarity Report that highlights matching text, source information, and an overall similarity
percentage.

    · Key characteristic 1: Text matching against internet sources, publications, and
       previously submitted work with source-level evidence.

    · Key characteristic 2: Similarity reports support academic review by grouping
       and presenting matched passages for human interpretation.

The limitations of Turnitin for the target FYP lifecycle are listed below.
    · Limitation 1: It primarily measures textual similarity and does not evaluate FYP
       problem, objectives, modules/features, methodology, implemented scope, and con-
       tribution boundaries as one semantic project comparison.

    · Limitation 2: It does not manage FYP team formation, supervisor allocation,
       coordinator routing, defense scheduling, project-progress intelligence, or final han-
       dover.

8.3 OpenProject [7]

    OpenProject is a general project-management and collaboration platform supporting
planning, tasks, milestones, workloads, timelines, and project progress.

    · Key characteristic 1: Work packages, tasks, milestones, timelines, and responsi-
       bilities can be planned and tracked across a project lifecycle.

    · Key characteristic 2: Team collaboration, workload planning, project dash-
       boards, and status tracking support coordinated project delivery.

The limitations of OpenProject for the target FYP lifecycle are listed below.
    · Limitation 1: It does not provide university-template proposal compliance, histor-
       ical FYP originality intelligence, or formal supervisor revision-compliance checking.

    · Limitation 2: It does not provide FYP-specific supervisor allocation, panel evalu-
       ation, institutional FYP archive reasoning, or GraphRAG-based knowledge search.
8.4 Microsoft Teams for Education [8]

    Microsoft Teams for Education combines class communication, channels, assignments,
due dates, resources, feedback, and rubric-based grading in one collaborative environ-
ment.

    · Key characteristic 1: Channels and class-team communication support collabo-
       ration among students and educators.

    · Key characteristic 2: Assignments support due dates, file submission, resubmis-
       sion, resources, grades, and rubrics.

The limitations of Microsoft Teams for Education for the target FYP lifecycle are listed
below.

    · Limitation 1: It does not provide FYP-specific teammate compatibility matching,
       supervisor expertise/capacity allocation, or historical FYP originality analysis.

    · Limitation 2: It does not manage coordinator academic routing, defense-panel
       optimization, project handover intelligence, or RAG/GraphRAG reasoning over
       FYP archives.

8.5 GitHub Projects [9]

    GitHub Projects supports software-team planning and tracking through issues, pull
requests, project boards, tables, roadmaps, custom fields, and code-linked work items.

    · Key characteristic 1: Issues and Projects can organize tasks, ideas, bugs, mile-
       stones, and development work with configurable views and metadata.

    · Key characteristic 2: Work tracking is closely linked with repositories, pull re-
       quests, and software-development activity.

The limitations of GitHub Projects for the target FYP lifecycle are listed below.
    · Limitation 1: It is development-work oriented and does not provide university
       proposal formatting/compliance, supervisor matching, or formal academic routing.

    · Limitation 2: It does not provide defense scheduling, panel rubric evaluation,
       semantic comparison with archived FYPs, or institutional FYP handover workflows.

8.6 DSpace Institutional Repository [10]

    DSpace is an institutional repository platform for managing, preserving, controlling
access to, and searching digital content and metadata.

    · Key characteristic 1: It preserves multiple digital formats and supports
       metadata/full-text search for institutional collections.

    · Key characteristic 2: Granular access control and repository organization sup-
       port long-term institutional knowledge preservation.

The limitations of DSpace for the target FYP lifecycle are listed below.
    · Limitation 1: It is primarily a repository and does not manage active FYP team
       formation, proposal review, supervisor allocation, progress, or defense workflows.

    · Limitation 2: Standard repository search does not provide FYP-specific seman-
       tic originality analysis, revision verification, or RAG/GraphRAG-based multi-hop
       academic reasoning by itself.
8.7 FYPHub (Our FYP)

    FYPHub combines the complete university FYP lifecycle in one role-aware platform
instead of treating submission, similarity checking, supervision, evaluation, progress, and
institutional knowledge as disconnected activities.

    · Key characteristic 1: Integrated 12-module workflow covering team formation,
       proposal compliance/originality, supervisor allocation/revision, coordinator control,
       defense/panel processes, project progress, final archive, and handover.

    · Key characteristic 2: AI-assisted institutional intelligence using LLMs, embed-
       dings, RAG/GraphRAG, optimization, and evidence-based analysis while retaining
       human academic authority.

9. Scope

    The scope of FYPHub covers the complete university FYP lifecycle through twelve
integrated modules. The system is software-based and uses role-aware workflows, de-
terministic academic rules, and AI-assisted analysis while preserving human academic
authority.

9.1 Included Aspects

    · Role-based web access for Student, Supervisor, FYP Coordinator, Panel Member,
       and Administrator users.
    · Student team formation, proposal submission/versioning, university-format com-
       pliance, and proposal originality analysis.

    · Supervisor matching/allocation, supervisor-led proposal review/revision, coordina-
       tor routing, defense scheduling, and panel evaluation.

    · Supervision evidence, milestone/progress monitoring, project-risk assistance, and
       recovery recommendations.

    · Final FYP archiving, handover/continuation knowledge, RAG/GraphRAG search,
       and institutional analytics.

9.2 Excluded Aspects

    · Automatic replacement of supervisor, coordinator, panel, or administrator academic
       decisions by AI; authorized personnel retain final authority.

    · University processes outside the FYP lifecycle such as general admissions, fee pro-
       cessing, and non-FYP academic administration.

    · Development of proprietary hardware or a native mobile application; the proposed
       system is a web-based software platform.

    · Use of confidential institutional or student data outside approved access permissions
       and role-based policies.

9.3 Modules Description

Module 1: Admin Dashboard & Academic Management
    Description: This module provides the administrative foundation of the platform.

It allows authorized administrators to manage users, roles, academic cycles, proposal
templates, supervisor capacity, evaluation rubrics, deadlines, reminders, escalation rules,
and audit history.

    · User and role management for Students, Supervisors, Coordinators, Panel Members,
       and Administrators.

    · Semester and FYP-cycle configuration including group-size and workflow policies.

    · Official short-proposal and long-proposal template management with versioned aca-
       demic rules.

    · Supervisor capacity, panel rubric, scoring criteria, and academic-deadline configu-
       ration.

    · Deadline reminder/escalation rules and immutable audit history of administrative
       changes.

Primary Output: Academic governance configuration and deadline action rules.

Module 2: Student Team & Collaboration Hub
    Description: This module manages student group formation and project collabo-

ration. It supports AI-assisted teammate discovery for students who do not yet have a
complete group and enforces the university rule that a student cannot belong to more
than one active FYP group in the same cycle.
    · FYP group creation, leader/member rules, group-size enforcement, and verified
       membership.

    · Duplicate-group membership prevention using Student ID/Registration Number
       and current FYP cycle.

    · AI teammate compatibility matching using 8-10 questions covering skills, domain,
       availability, working style, preferred role, and commitment.

    · Join requests, invitations, accept/reject workflow, student-to-group matching, and
       temporary match chat.

    · Seven collaboration channels (General, Proposal, Research, Supervisor Discussion,
       Tasks & Progress, Files & Resources, Announcements & Deadlines) plus a group
       AI chatbot.

Primary Output: Verified and compatible FYP team with a shared collaboration
workspace.

Module 3: Student Proposal & AI Compliance Check
    Description: This module owns the official proposal record and validates a student's

short or long proposal against the university template and academic-quality rules. The
same analysis can be viewed by authorized supervisors, coordinators, and panel members
according to role permissions.

    · Short/long proposal creation, upload, draft, submit, resubmit, and lifecycle-status
       management.

    · Version history with AI-generated change summaries between proposal versions.

    · University-format checking for font, font size, margins, alignment, line spacing,
       heading styles, page numbering, tables, and figures.

    · Structural validation for required sections, section order, headings, references, ap-
       pendices, and captions.

    · Academic consistency analysis across problem statement, objectives, methodology,
       proposed solution, scope, and expected outcomes.

Primary Output: Official proposal version and Proposal Compliance & Readiness Re-
port.

Module 4: Proposal Similarity & Originality Check
    Description: This module compares a new proposal with archived FYPs to deter-

mine how much of the idea, problem, implementation scope, and methodology overlaps
with previous work and where a genuine contribution can begin.

    · Semantic embedding search against historical FYP records.

    · Multi-level comparison of title, problem statement, objectives, modules/features,
       and methodology.

    · Evidence showing the most related previous projects and their implemented scope.
    · Contribution-boundary analysis separating inherited/overlapping work from gen-
       uinely new work.

    · Gap and extension analysis based on previous limitations, missing features, and
       unfinished scope.

Primary Output: Originality, duplication, and contribution-boundary report.

Module 5: Supervisor Selection & Allocation
    Description: This module identifies suitable supervisors and manages the complete

assignment process using expertise relevance, research interests, current workload, capac-
ity, and academic constraints.

    · AI-based supervisor expertise matching using proposal content, research interests,
       and previous supervision history.

    · Ranked supervisor recommendations with explainable match reasons and expertise
       gaps.

    · Supervisor capacity and slot management showing total, filled, and available slots.

    · Student request, supervisor accept/reject, duplicate-request protection, and final
       assignment workflow.

    · Fair allocation and conflict checking using workload, preferences, capacity, and
       academic constraints.

Primary Output: Explainable supervisor recommendation and valid final assignment.

Module 6: Supervisor Proposal Review Dashboard
    Description: After assignment, this module manages the formal supervisor-led pro-

posal review process. Comments and requested changes remain linked to proposal versions
so that the academic revision history is fully traceable.

    · Paragraph-level, section-level, module-level, and full-proposal comments.

    · Editing suggestions and formal revision requests from the assigned supervisor.

    · Two-way student-supervisor discussion linked to the relevant proposal/revision
       item.

    · Semantic comparison between old and revised proposal versions.

    · AI revision-compliance checking that classifies requested changes as Resolved, Par-
       tially Resolved, or Unresolved while leaving final authority with the supervisor.

Primary Output: Revision Resolution Report and approved/revised proposal history.

Module 7: Coordinator Proposal Control
    Description: This module gives the FYP Coordinator controlled academic routing

over supervisor-approved proposals and provides visibility into delayed, incomplete, or
workflow-inconsistent cases.

    · Dedicated queue of supervisor-approved proposals ready for coordinator action.
    · Hold, return-to-student, return-to-supervisor, and forward-to-panel actions.

    · Version-aware, immutable history of coordinator decisions and reasons.

    · Workflow bottleneck and abnormal-delay detection across proposal stages.

    · Priority and conformance monitoring for urgent cases, skipped stages, unresolved
       requirements, and deadline risk.

Primary Output: Coordinator decision record and panel-ready proposal state.

Module 8: Panel Scheduling & Defense
    Description: This module manages defense logistics after a proposal/project be-

comes eligible for panel evaluation. It allocates panel members, time slots, and rooms
while preventing scheduling and academic conflicts.

    · Panel-member assignment for eligible FYP groups.

    · Availability management for panel members, supervisors, student groups, and
       rooms.

    · Defense date, time, room, or online-session scheduling.

    · Automatic detection of panel, supervisor, student, room, and conflict-of-interest
       clashes.

    · Optimized scheduling and rescheduling to reduce conflicts and balance panel work-
       load.

Primary Output: Conflict-free defense schedule and panel assignment.

Module 9: Panel Evaluation & Feedback
    Description: This module manages formal panel evaluation using configured rubrics.

It supports detailed feedback, AI-assisted defense preparation, evaluator-disagreement
detection, and traceable feedback resolution.

    · Criterion-based rubric scoring and recommendation by multiple panel members.

    · Paragraph, section, module/feature, and overall proposal/project comments.

    · AI-generated viva/defense questions based on proposal content and claimed contri-
       bution.

    · Detection of significant evaluator disagreement in scores or assessment dimensions.

    · Feedback-resolution workflow: Panel Comment  Coordinator Resolution  Stu-
       dent/Supervisor Revision  Panel Recheck, while preserving the original panel
       comment.

Primary Output: Structured panel evaluation, feedback history, and resolution status.

Module 10: Project Progress & Supervision Dashboard
    Description: This module combines formal supervision evidence with milestone and

task progress to present the current health of an active FYP and identify delay or delivery
risks.
    · Supervision meeting records, minutes, decisions, action items, owners, and due
       dates.

    · AI extraction of tasks, owners, commitments, and deadlines from meeting notes.

    · Milestone, task, completion-percentage, overdue-work, and scope-progress tracking.

    · Project-health classification such as On Track, Attention Required, or Critical using
       recorded evidence.

    · Risk explanation and recovery recommendations; when sufficient labelled data be-
       comes available, predictive ML models and explainability can be added for delay
       probability estimation.

Primary Output: Project Progress, Action Compliance, Risk, and Recovery Report.

Module 11: Final FYP Archive & Handover
    Description: This module creates the final institutional record of a completed FYP

and prepares reusable knowledge for future students instead of treating the final report
as a static file.

    · Final project submission package containing report, proposal, source-code reposi-
       tory, dataset/model information, and supporting files.

    · Automatic extraction of project metadata including domain, technologies, datasets,
       modules, and methodology.

    · Classification of final scope as Completed, Partially Implemented, or Not Imple-
       mented.

    · Extraction of limitations, unresolved issues, technical debt, and future work.

    · Intelligent handover/continuation package containing reusable assets, dependencies,
       incomplete work, and recommended continuation points.

Primary Output: Final Project Archive Package and Continuation/Handover Package.

Module 12: FYP Knowledge Search & Analytics
    Description: This module reads and reasons over archived institutional FYP knowl-

edge. It provides evidence-based natural-language search for authorized users and pro-
duces institutional analytics without editing the underlying archive.

    · Natural-language semantic search over archived FYP titles, abstracts, modules,
       technologies, datasets, outcomes, and limitations.

    · RAG and GraphRAG-based question answering grounded in retrieved institutional
       evidence.

    · Multi-hop queries across relationships such as Project  Technology  Dataset 
       Limitation  Supervisor.

    · Source citations/evidence paths so generated answers can be traced to archived
       FYP records.
· Institutional analytics for domain trends, supervisor workload, approval turnaround,
  completion/delay patterns, panel statistics, and technology usage over time.

Primary Output: Evidence-Based FYP Knowledge Answers and Institutional Analyt-
ics Report.

12. Significance

    FYPHub is significant because it transforms the university's FYP process from a
collection of disconnected administrative tasks into a connected academic intelligence
ecosystem. The platform does not only digitize proposal forms; it links team formation,
proposal quality, originality, supervision, coordinator routing, scheduling, panel evalua-
tion, progress evidence, final archiving, and institutional knowledge reuse.

    The main expected benefits are:

· Improved Proposal Quality: Students receive structured feedback on university
  formatting, missing sections, academic consistency, and readiness before formal
  review.

· Reduced Duplication: Semantic and functional comparison against previous
  FYPs reveals overlap and clarifies where genuine new contribution should begin.

· Better Team Formation: AI teammate matching and duplicate-membership
  rules support fair and compatible group creation.

· Transparent Supervision: Supervisor selection considers expertise and capacity,
  while revision comments and meeting actions remain traceable.

· Efficient Academic Routing: Coordinators can monitor proposal states, delays,
  and panel readiness from one controlled workflow.

· Improved Defense Management: Panel assignments and defense schedules are
  generated with conflict and availability checks.

· Evidence-Based Progress Monitoring: Meeting actions, milestones, tasks, and
  delays contribute to project-health and recovery recommendations.

· Institutional Knowledge Reuse: Final FYPs become searchable, reusable
  knowledge through RAG/GraphRAG and analytics rather than remaining isolated
  documents.
    · Human-Controlled AI: AI supports analysis, explanation, retrieval, prediction,
       and recommendations, while authorized academic personnel retain final decision
       authority.

13. Tools and Techniques

    FYPHub uses a modular web and AI architecture that separates deterministic aca-
demic rules from probabilistic AI assistance. The major tools and techniques are listed
below.

    · Front-End: React + Vite + TypeScript with Tailwind CSS will provide responsive
       role-specific interfaces for Student, Supervisor, Coordinator, Panel, and Adminis-
       trator users [11].

    · Back-End: Python 3.12 with FastAPI will provide authenticated REST APIs, role-
       based authorization, academic workflow rules, AI-service endpoints, and integration
       logic [12].

    · Database: PostgreSQL will store users, teams, proposals, versions, assignments,
       reviews, coordinator actions, schedules, evaluations, progress records, and archive
       metadata [13]. The pgvector extension will support proposal embeddings, semantic
       retrieval, and historical FYP similarity search [14].

    · AI and Intelligence: LLMs and embeddings will support proposal-quality anal-
       ysis, semantic revision checking, teammate explanations, viva-question generation,
       summarization, and semantic search. RAG will ground generated answers in re-
       trieved institutional evidence [3], while GraphRAG will add graph-structured re-
       trieval for broader and multi-hop reasoning across archived FYP relationships [4].
       Optimization techniques will support supervisor allocation and defense scheduling,
       while project-risk analysis will begin with deterministic/hybrid evidence and may
       later use supervised ML when sufficient labelled data becomes available.

    · Security and Academic Rules: Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) and de-
       terministic backend/database constraints will enforce group uniqueness, supervisor
       slots, proposal-version eligibility, coordinator routing, panel permissions, and ad-
       ministrative policies rather than delegating these controls to an LLM.

    · Development Tools: Docker and Docker Compose will support reproducible
       infrastructure [15]; Git/GitHub will support collaborative version control; Swag-
       ger/OpenAPI will support API documentation and testing; Ollama will support
       local AI model execution.

    Overall Workflow: Admin Setup  Team Formation  Proposal Submission &
AI Compliance  Similarity & Originality Check  Supervisor Selection & Allocation
 Supervisor Review & Revision  Coordinator Proposal Control  Defense & Panel
Scheduling  Panel Evaluation & Feedback  Project Progress & Supervision  Final
FYP Archive & Handover  FYP Knowledge Search & Analytics.
